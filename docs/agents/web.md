# Web app (`public/`)

Reference notes for agents, split out of `AGENTS.md`. Read this before working in the area it covers, and update it in the same change as the code it describes.

Dependency-free vanilla JS as native ES modules, with no build step.

## `app.js`: state, views and routes

- `app.js` — the module entry (`<script type="module">`): app state, `shell()`, the views and the hash router (`#/`, `#/new`, `#/event/:id`, `#/event/:id/edit`, `#/track/:id`, `#/track/:id/compare` — the two-event lap overlay — `#/track/:id/lap-compare` — the cross-event two-lap telemetry compare (#165): picker, head-to-head table, delta + channel charts over an `alignLapPair` pair — `#/track/:id/leaderboard` — the track's leaderboard, its own page behind a *Leaderboard* button on the track page rather than a section of it (the page is the driver's own history and the board was costing it a screen of space; a driver who does care wants it before they are on it), offered for every catalog track and carrying the opt-in controls — `#/track/:id/leaderboard/:lapId` — one leaderboard row, opened (NS-35): another driver's ranked lap, its racing line and traces, with the viewer's own best at the track laid over it through the *same* `alignLapPair` pair, defaulting to their fastest and picked with `?mine=`. Their lap is always side A, so it keeps its colour whether or not there is one to put beside it, and the readout both views share is `bindPairTooltip`, which takes its sides from `sideLabels` and so needs no special case for a one-sided render — `#/garage` — the Garage (NS-37, `docs/specs/native/NS-37-garage-tab.md`), the other half of the app beside the logbook and reached from the top bar's *Events* / *Garage* links (`sectionNavHtml`; the *Garage* link carries the Pro count of maintenance reminders, refreshed from `/garage` in the background at most once a minute by `refreshGarageBadge` and reported directly by any page that already read it through `noteGarage`): a card per car from the free `GET /vehicles` with `vehicleTileLine`, plus hours and the worst part's status for Pro, the maintenance strip for Pro, and a *+ Add car* tile — disabled offline, since vehicle writes are off the queue — that opens the add form with the catalog picker; a free account sees the Pro half as one locked panel. Cars are managed here and nowhere else — `#/vehicle/:id` — the garage page, **free** since NS-37: the car's logbook (track days, events, last/next event, best lap per track — `vehicleLogbook` over the cached `/events`) and its *Edit car* form (with *Delete car*, which used to be in Settings) for every account, and for Pro above it the consumables with wear bars/projections — split into *On the car* and *Spares*, each card carrying an **Equipped** switch whose confirm row takes the swap date and names what `equipSwapsOff` will take off (on all three clients: both native vehicle pages carry the sections, the switch — a confirm sheet on iOS (one case of the page's single `.sheet`), an inline confirm row on Android as on the web — an `OutlinedTextField` in an `AlertDialog` never lets Robolectric idle — never a write on its own — the size field and the retired Refresh, over `Garage.equipSwapKinds` / `equipSwapsOff` / `partTitle` / `equipNote` in the Kit and `:core`, pinned by `garage-status.json`) — measurement logging, the add-part form (size, and an Equipped box that sends `swap: true`), and the *Edit car* form (name, modifications & notes, target hot pressure, default) — the per-event **track-hours ledger it used to end with is gone** on every client (2026-09): the tile carries the total and each event's own form carries its override, so the page fetches `/garage` alone — `#/settings` — units, the prep-checklist template, leaderboard consents, the subscription and the privacy/terms links, reached from the account dropdown; its Vehicles section is gone, since the Garage has its own button (NS-37) — `#/wrapped/:year` — Season Wrapped (NS-36), the season as a story of full-viewport cards over `GET /api/wrapped/:year`, `#/wrapped` alone opening the newest year and a year with no track days rendering a page with the year picker rather than the error banner; the story is a fixed layer over the shell, so the shell still renders under it — `#/coaching`, `#/profile`, `#/coach` and `#/student/:id/…` — share with a coach (NS-38): the Coaching page (invites shown once and withdrawn, coaches with `lastViewedText` and Remove, the drivers you coach) reached from the account menu and Settings, the driver-profile form, the page an invite link lands on, and a student's logbook read-only.

**An invite link is `/coach/<token>`**: the SPA shell answers it, and the boot code moves the token into `sessionStorage` (`COACH_INVITE_KEY`) and `replaceState`s to `/#/coach` before `route()` runs, so it survives a sign-in round trip and never sits in history; `route()` sends any signed-in page there while one is pending, and `sw.js` caches neither `/coach/*` nor `/api/coaching/invites/*`.

**The student view reuses the owner's pages rather than copying them** — `viewEvent`, `viewTrack`, `viewCompare`, `viewLapCompare` and `viewProGate` — through three module-level seams set by `routeStudent`: `viewing` (the student, from their `/me/profile` under the coach mount; `route()` resets it to null on every navigation), which makes `api()` prefix every read with `/students/<id>` and refuse a write before sending it; `L(hash)`, which every link and `location.hash` in those pages goes through so navigation stays inside the student's logbook; and `ent()`, the entitlement the channel panel and the Pro gates read — the **student's** tier, not the viewer's. Each of those pages takes `readOnly()` to drop its write controls, the setup notebook and the checklist (neither is shared) and the PB celebration; a new write control on one of them owes that check, and a new link owes `L()`. The student's dashboard and car page are small dedicated views (`viewStudentHome`, `viewStudentVehicle`), since the owner's carry garage and share controls on every line. A 404 from the student's `/me/profile` means the grant is gone: `forgetStudent` clears the student's responses from the offline cache (`removeCachedPrefix`) and the service worker's `th-data-*` caches — plus public `/share/<slug>` pages with their own hash routes, and `/share/(e|s)/<token>` — one shared event or session (NS-48), which is `viewEvent` itself rendered read-only from the public payload through a fourth seam, `sharedView` (`{ kind, prefix, token, name, pro, event }`, set by `shareItemRoute`): it makes `readOnly()` true, `ent()` the **owner's** tier, the page render into `shareShell`, the channel panel's free-half note `sharedFreeNote`, and `openLapVideo` fetch `sharedClipUrl` instead of `/api/clips/:id/url`; viewEvent then reads no `/api` route at all. The owner's half is `bindItemShare` — *Share* beside *Edit event* and beside each session's *Edit*, opening an inline panel with the link, *Copy*, `navigator.share` where it exists and *Stop sharing*; the panel is `bindSharePanel` + `showShareLink`, and since NS-56 a session's carries `shareChooser` — *Share* [this session / Lap N] and, for a lap with telemetry on an account with the compare, *compared with* [Lap M] — where a lap or pair is only **read** (`GET`, past the cache) and offers *Create link* when it has none, so browsing the chooser never publishes a lap. `#/track/:id/lap-compare` has its own *Share* for the picked pair. `/share/l/<token>` is `viewEvent` through the same seam; `/share/c/<token>` is `viewSharedCompare`, which hands the two sides to `renderLapPair` — the compare page below its heading, split out of `viewLapCompare` and taking `[{ row, session }]` rather than a session-id map, because a shared pair's two one-lap sessions may be the same session). The event page carries the per-day setup notebook (copy-forward prefill, changed-value highlighting), the track page a "Setup vs. lap times" diff table, and the dashboard's next-event hero one line naming the most urgent part on that event's car (`heroGarageHtml`, Pro, from `GET /api/garage`) — the garage cards and maintenance strip it used to carry moved to `#/garage`.

**Adding anything starts from one + in the top bar** — a menu of *Add event*, *Import laps* and *Record laps* (the web's `#add-trigger` in `shell()`, on every page, whose *Record laps* points at `/get` since the recorder is the phones'; iOS's `addMenu` toolbar `Menu` and Android's `DashboardTopBar` dropdown, on the dashboard, with *Record laps* only while the recorder is idle and never on a Mac) — and the dashboards carry no add buttons of their own beyond the first-run welcome card's. Three layout rules the three clients share, and which a change to any one of them owes the other two: **a track card is its name, its best lap and its counts — and no trend line**, because a track usually holds two or three events and a two-point sparkline is a dot and a dot pretending to be a season (the `series` the API still sends for it is now decoded by nobody — `src/db.ts` says why it stays); **the dashboard lists upcoming events and tracks — no recent-events table, and no garage, which is its own tab / page since NS-37**, because the Tracks grid already orders by recency and the table was the same logbook said twice (the one thing it used to be load-bearing for is written up under *Offline support* in README); and **every way of getting laps into an event is one "Add a session" card** at the bottom of the event page — import, hand entry, and on the native clients the recorder — rather than a stack of separate panels, with hand entry collapsed behind a button since it is the fallback of the three. The **New Event form carries the same two non-recorder doors as an "Add laps" section** (new events only, never the edit form): the import review and a hand-entry session are *staged* on the form and posted after `POST /events`, in the order `sessionsToCreate` in `public/js/event-form.js` fixes — staged imports first, then the typed session, dropped when no lap parses — ported as `EventFormSessions` to the Kit and `:core` and pinned by `contracts/logic/event-form.json`; the created id is remembered so a rejected session is retried without a second event. On the web `bindTelemetryImport` takes `options.onSessions` for this and hands back the same bodies `importSessionBody` builds for the event page; on the phones the importer's route carries `forNewEvent` (`Route.importVideo(forNewEvent:)` / `Route.Import(forNewEvent)`), the review drops its event picker, and its save hands the drafts back through `AppRouter.stagedSessions` / `RecordingFlow.staged` rather than posting them. Each route re-renders the whole view via `shell()`, so element handles go stale after any navigation; module-level listeners are used where per-render listeners would accumulate. HTML is built with template strings — always pipe user data through `esc()`.

## Telemetry import (`public/js/import/`, `public/pdr.js`)

- `js/import/` — the telemetry import feature: `channels.js` builds the per-lap channel data stored with imported sessions (`sessions.channels` JSON; the shape is validated server-side by `sanitizeChannels` in `src/lib/validate.ts` — keep the two in sync).

**Sample rate picks the storage shape**, and a PDR file's 66 channels make the split obvious: anything ≥ ~5 Hz is a *gridded trace* resampled onto a 20 m driven-distance grid (`CHANNEL_NAMES`: speed, rpm, latG, throttle, brake, steering, longG, yaw, gear, wheelSlip, boost, flags), anything slower produces one real sample every 40–90 m and so becomes a *per-lap scalar* instead (`SCALAR_NAMES`: oil/coolant/trans temps, oil pressure, fuel, battery, tire pressures ×4 and temps ×4, each with its own `max`/`min`/`end` reduction), and four numbers describing the whole recording become the blob's `meta` (`META_NAMES`: ambientC, intakeC, elevationM, odometerKm — the last is the *car's lifetime odometer*, not the session's distance). Four rules there are load-bearing. `CHANNEL_NAMES` order is both render order *and* priority: the six original channels keep their positions so existing graphs are unchanged, and `trimToBudget` drops from the **tail** until a session fits `MAX_TOTAL_VALUES` rather than returning null and losing every graph — which at 12 channels is now reachable (~38 VIR laps, where 6 channels never got close). And two channels are states, not measurements, so they get their own samplers: `STEP_HOLD` (`gear` — interpolating 3 and 4 yields 3.5) and `WINDOW_MAX` (`flags` — a half-second ABS event is narrower than 20 m and a point sample misses it). And nothing is read past a channel's own ends: a lap may run up to 5 s beyond them and `series.at` extrapolates, so the interpolated sampler and the `end` scalar clamp to the series (`clampT`) — an extrapolated 100.5 % throttle or −0.004 G is a `sanitizeChannels` 400 that loses the whole session. They are captured at import because the video never leaves the device, so a reading not derived then is gone; `parse.js` dispatches a dropped file to a parser (`.vbo` → `vbo.js`, `.csv` → `csv.js`, `.mp4` → the Corvette's Marlin PDR first, then the newer AliveDrive PDR 2.5 (`pdr25.js`, below) only on a Marlin *no track*, then GoPro `gpmf.js`); `vbo.js` reads both `[column names]` layouts — one space-separated line (VBOX) or one name per line (Porsche Track Precision, whose names contain spaces) — negates Racelogic's west-positive longitude so the racing line isn't mirrored, reads the `[laptiming]` line in either endpoint order (whichever lies on the trace), widens it to a 40 m direction-filtered gate (`widenGate`; Track Precision's is ~15 m and off to one side) and extrapolates a crossing for a first/last fix within `EDGE_M` of it (`edgeCrossings`; Track Precision starts and stops recording at the line), and returns `carChannels` / `lapScalarChannels` / `sessionMeta` like PDR does — which `channelDataFor` now reads for any source that sets `carChannels`, not only `pdr`. Unit traps there: Track Precision's `latacc`/`longacc` columns are G ÷ 9.81 on current firmware (the `_PTPA` columns are preferred) but the `_PTPA` columns are themselves **m/s² on 2024 firmware**, so `accelToG` picks the unit per file from the 99th-percentile magnitude; brake is a *pressure*, stored as % of the file's own peak; and a car that stops reporting mid-session keeps writing every car column as exactly 0, so a row at 0 rpm faster than `CAR_SILENT_MS` (`carSilent`) contributes no car values. The finishing of those series is `finishCarChannels`, exported because `csv.js` shares it: Porsche Track Precision's CSV export — the same car's channels in a camelCase-header layout with epoch-ms `timestamp` and decimal degrees — whose laps come from **the app's own lap timer** (`lapsFromLaptime`: crossing = `timestamp − laptime`, a lap kept only when its counter ran the whole way, the last lap of a recording stopped at the line extrapolated from `lapDistance` only at pace, since the pit-lane cool-down reaches a lap's distance too), exact rather than estimated and needing no line, falling back to the picker when the timer never completes a lap; its `speed` is km/h or m/s by firmware, decided against the GPS by `speedToMs`. Ported as `TrackPrecisionCsv` to the Kit and `:core`, pinned by `contracts/logic/csv-parsers.json` over the committed files in `contracts/logic/csv/` plus timer tables for `lapsFromLaptime`; every parser returns `{kind, date, time, durationS, laps, gps, needsLine}`. `geo.js` projects GPS traces to local meters and derives laps from start/finish line crossings — and, since NS-53, carries the pure half of drawing a stored trace on a real map: `traceOrigin` (the anchor a save sends — every parser sets it as `traceGeo` beside `bestLapTrace`, `importSessionBody` sends it as `trace_geo`, and a batch item that only fitted the picked line through the longitude mirror gets null, since no origin describes a trace projected from negated longitudes), `unprojectTrace`, `traceGeoBounds`, `nearestOnTrace` (a tap's hit test, by distance driven rather than index) and `speedRuns` (the line cut into runs of one speed colour), ported under the same names to `Geo` in the Kit and `GeoTrace` in `:core` and pinned by `contracts/logic/geo-anchor.json`; the three parser fixtures pin `traceGeo` too; `pdr-laps.js` recovers laps for beacon-less PDR recordings from latitude+odometer periodicity (autocorrelation lap length; start/finish phase anchored to a beacon-timed batch-mate via `anchorPdrBatch`); `ui.js` is the dropzone/review flow, including the click-a-map line picker for GPS sources without lap markers — GoPro, plain VBO (one picked line applies to the whole batch, with automatic longitude-sign mirroring for Racelogic's west-positive convention). Test fixtures for all formats are generated by `test/fixtures/build.mjs`. Real sample recordings for manual import testing live in the gitignored `telemetry-samples/` at the repo root — never commit footage.

- `js/import/locate.js` + `js/import/auto.js` — import laps with no event picked (NS-57, `docs/specs/native/NS-57-auto-place-laps.md`; **built on all three clients (tickets 1–4), except the catalog's curated start/finish lines; the iOS app half was written without Xcode and is unverified until CI builds it**). `locate.js` is the **pure half, ported as `TrackLocate`** to the Kit (`Telemetry/TrackLocate.swift`, inputs as three small structs built from the models) and `:core` (`TrackLocate.kt`, inputs as the interfaces `OwnTrack` / `CatalogRow` / `EventRow` the models implement) under the same names and pinned by `contracts/logic/track-locate.json` (`TrackLocateTests` / `TrackLocateTest`): `OWN_RADIUS_M` (400) / `VENUE_RADIUS_M` (2000) / `LENGTH_TOLERANCE` (0.04) / `DATE_SLACK_DAYS` (1), `distanceM` (on `projectTrace`'s `KX` / `KY`), `traceLengthM` (the server's `polylineLength`), `recordingPlace(parsed)` — `{ lat, lon, lengthM }`, the bounding-box centre of `bestLapTrace` through `traceGeo` and its closed length, or the raw `gps` centre with `lengthM: null` for a file still waiting for its line — `locateTrack(place, ownTracks, catalog)` → `{ confidence: "exact" | "choose" | "none", candidates }`, `eventForDate`, `groupImports(items, events, { choices, ownTracks, today })` → the review's cards, `eventBodyFor`, `otherTrackHere` and `groupDatesText`. Four rules are load-bearing.

**Matching runs on the device**: no coordinate goes anywhere to be matched.

**The driver's own tracks come first** — within `OWN_RADIUS_M` and `LENGTH_TOLERANCE` (4 %, either way) of a track's `geo` — so a track named once is recognised ever after with no catalog row; then catalog rows within `VENUE_RADIUS_M` whose length the lap does not rule out (`fitsCatalogLength`: up to `CATALOG_SHORT_TOLERANCE`, 6 %, short of the published length and `CATALOG_LONG_TOLERANCE`, 2 %, long — on real footage a best-lap trace ran 0.7–4.0 % short at every venue and never long), a row the driver already has a track for coming back as that track.

**Location finds the venue and only length finds the layout**: `exact` needs exactly one candidate *with a length that agrees*, so a NULL `length_m`, a place with no laps yet, or two layouts the tolerance cannot separate is always `choose`, and `test/unit/locate.test.js` fails if a new catalog row makes two layouts at one venue indistinguishable (their length windows overlap).

**A venue we know with a layout we don't is still offered**: when length rules out every catalog row in the radius, they come back as a `choose` with `venueOnly: true` and the card says the lap fits no layout known there, so a driver on a configuration the catalog lacks (Mid-Ohio's 2.258-mile course) picks the venue or names the layout instead of being told nothing is there; `none` means no row in the radius at all.

**A file joins an existing event only at the same track**, one whose days cover its date — the one-day slack (file clocks are local on some sources, UTC on others) is consulted only when none does — else it groups by track and run of consecutive dates into a new event. `auto.js` is the web-only review behind `#/import` (`viewImport` in `app.js`, reached from *Import laps* in the top bar's + menu and on the welcome card; `bindTelemetryImport`'s `options.auto` hands it the parsed results in place of `reviewResults`): one card per group, never saved unasked, a `choose` card opening on *Pick the track…* rather than a pre-selected layout, an unknown place named once for every date at it, one line picker per venue for the files that need one (a file joins the cards once its line gives it laps), and a save that is `POST /events` then each session through `api()` — so the offline queue, temp ids and the clip hand-off are the New Event form's. A new group whose event was created before a session failed keeps that event id on its items, so a retry never creates a second event. On the event page the review gains one line through `options.locate` when a file was certainly (`exact`) driven at another track. The server's half: migration `0040_track_catalog_geo.sql` (`track_catalog.lat` / `lon`, the centre of OpenStreetMap's bounding box for each circuit or venue; the seven rows OSM has no object of their own for are commented in the file and sit inside the venue it does map), `GET /api/catalog` answering `{ id, name, lat, lon, length_m }`, and `geo` (`trackPlace` in `lib/geo.ts`: `{ lat, lon, length_m }` or null, from `trackPlacesStmt` — the newest anchored session per track, in the route's one batch) on each row of the owner's `GET /api/tracks`. `geo` is added in the route and **not** in `summarizeTracks`, which the share page also builds from, and it is absent from `COACH_TRACK_FIELDS` and the MCP `list_tracks` result: a track's place is as private as `trace_geo` (`test/api/track-geo.test.ts`). In `locate.js`, `knownLines(place, ownTracks)` offers the own tracks within `VENUE_RADIUS_M` that have one, `gateFromSf(sf, origin)` rebuilds `buildGate`'s shape in the file's own frame (direction filter kept, so a reversed day finds nothing), and `lineFits(trackId, places, ownTracks, catalog)` keeps the line only when the laps it cut locate `exact` to that own track — so another layout's line is never kept. `auto.js` tries it per venue picker and `ui.js` on an event whose track has one (`options.locate`); both say *"Start/finish from your last session at …"* and keep the map.

- `pdr.js` — parses lap times, the GPS trace, and car metrics (top speed / max RPM / max lateral G) out of Corvette PDR (Cosworth) MP4 telemetry entirely in the browser via byte-range reads; the video is never uploaded. The beacon/odometer lap-derivation logic is reverse-engineered — see README before touching it. The stream is **delta-encoded** (framing matches ExifTool's GM.pm): each channel gets one full record, then 8-byte diffs against running decoder state that persists across samples — decoding the deltas is what yields ~11Hz lat/lon (radians, scaled via the `mrld` channel dictionary) and the car channels (steering is radians with an empty units string in real firmware — pdr.js converts to degrees itself). Which of the file's 66 channels are read is the `CHANNEL_TAGS` table, which also records what is deliberately *not* read (recorder CPU/disk housekeeping, session constants, redundant or derivable channels, and the suspension/clutch channels whose scaling needs a second car to validate). Three of the units in `UNIT_CONV` are the ones that bite: the dictionary names a **display** unit while storing SI, so `°C` channels hold **Kelvin** — which is why the table is `[factor, offset]` and not just a factor; ship a temperature without the offset and 130 °C oil reads as 403 °C — `kPa` channels hold **Pascals**, and `km` channels hold **metres**. The recording odometer is deliberately *not* run through `UNIT_CONV`: `odoPts` stays raw because it is the driven-distance axis for `channels.js`, not a displayed value. `gear` is an enum, not a measurement (1–8 are gears; 13 is the clutch-in state, mapped to 0 — never plot it as a thirteenth gear), `wheelSlip` is derived from the four wheelspeeds as one channel rather than four, and ABS/traction/stability are packed into one `flags` bitfield. Coordinates only become a `gps` trace behind plausibility checks; when they don't decode, the raw latitude/odometer series returned as `channels` still feed lap recovery in `js/import/pdr-laps.js`.

- `js/import/pdr25.js` — the **newer** Cosworth recorder, "AliveDrive PDR 2.5" (2025-on GM cars; first seen on a Cadillac CT5-V Blackwing): a different format, not a revision of `pdr.js`'s — track handler `adrv`, sample entry `adco` — that describes itself completely (`adcp` channel table with SI scalings and enum labels, `adcr` record schedule, `adeg` event names), so nothing is hard-coded but the Cosworth channel names in `CHANNELS_25`. The sample stream is 1-second data blocks of **untagged** fixed-width records interleaved by the schedule (each group writes at the ticks its period divides), plus 11-byte event blocks; laps are the recorder's `lap.start` / `lap.end` events, exact. It resolves to exactly `parsePdrFile`'s shape and `kind: "pdr"` — same display units, `channels: null` (no recording odometer, so `pdr-laps.js` never runs on it) — so nothing downstream distinguishes the two. Three traps, stated in its header and inherited by the `PDR25` ports in the Kit and `:core`: `accelerometer.vehicle.x` is **lateral** and `vehicle.y` **longitudinal with braking positive** (measured, not named); enums are read by **label** (the stability-enhancement channel numbers active as 0); and the outing `timestamp` says `+00:00` but is **local** wall-clock time. Pinned by `contracts/logic/video-parsers.json`'s `pdr25-laps.mp4` / `pdr25-nolaps.mp4`, built by `buildPdr25Mp4` in `test/fixtures/build.mjs` at lower rates than the real recorder's so the committed files stay small; real Blackwing footage lives in the gitignored `telemetry-samples/pdr-caddy/`.

- `js/import/smartycam.js` — **AiM SmartyCam** video (NS-55, `docs/specs/native/NS-55-smartycam-import.md`; **on all three clients** since tickets 2 and 3 — ported as `SmartyCam` to the Kit (`Telemetry/SmartyCam.swift`, `parseSmartyCamFile`) and `:core` (`telemetry/SmartyCam.kt`) under the JS names, reading through `TelemetryByteSource`, with the JS cases in `SmartyCamTests` / `SmartyCamTest` and the fixture asserted by `SmartyCamContractTests` / `SmartyCamContractTest`; both dispatches (`Telemetry.swift` / `Telemetry.kt`) try it where `parse.js` does and carry its three error choices, both `Kind` enums gain `smartycam` (label *SmartyCam*), and both `LapChannels` ports count it among the kinds with car channels — without which rpm / throttle / gear never reach the per-lap arrays. The clip cutters' rule is the spec's ticket 4, so `VIDEO_KINDS` does not list it on any client and no clip is offered; a port must not add it there early. Android's `ContentByteSource.nameForType` gives a bare shared name typed `video/quicktime` a `.mov`, and iOS's `-importFixture` also looks in `contracts/logic/smartycam/`). A SmartyCam `.MOV` is QuickTime with a third track — handler `free`, declared as `sowt` PCM audio — that is the telemetry: one chunk per five video frames (6 Hz), of which the first `RECORD_BYTES` (124) are a little-endian record and the rest noise; the chunk length is read from the file, never assumed (the camera's are 16,000 bytes, the fixtures' 136). It resolves to `kind: "smartycam"` with `laps`, `gps`, `carChannels` (`rpm`, `throttle`, `gear`), `metrics`, `bestLapTrace` / `traceGeo` and `needsLine: false`, and `parse.js` tries it after the two PDRs and before GoPro, for `.mp4` and `.mov` alike. Four rules are load-bearing and a port inherits each.

**`t` is video time, `frame / fps`**: the camera drops frames (about 3 s at the start of every file, up to 3 % after), so a lap is shorter in the file than on the clock; with video time every `startT` / `endT` cuts a clip where the lap is, and **lap times are the logger's own field, never `endT − startT`** — which is also why there is no line-picker fallback, since a picked line would time laps on the video clock.

**A lap is two consecutive steps of the logger's lap counter** (`lapsFromCounter`), each boundary `LAP_LATENCY_RECORDS` (1) before its step, because the counter trails the crossing; the first step in a file closes the out-lap and only opens the next lap, a counter that jumps, resets or goes missing opens nothing, and a lap whose logger-clock length is more than `LAP_CLOCK_TOLERANCE_MS` from its reported time is dropped.

**Position is the ECEF fix** through `ecefToGeodetic` (five fixed iterations, so ports agree to the digit), never the ×1e7 latitude / longitude beside it, which refresh at 2 Hz and trail by up to 45 m. And **each car field is kept only if it passes `plausibleChannels`**, because their meaning is confirmed on one camera configuration; a failing field is dropped and the import never fails on it. `-12290` (`NO_VALUE`) is the camera's "no reading". Only the date and time are read from the trailing `<aim_meta_data>` XML — never the driver's name, the vehicle or a serial, and never its `nlap` / `best`, which are the logger's totals for the day. A `.mov` with no `moov` (the camera lost power; it leaves a `.FOF` beside it) is *"This recording wasn't finished by the camera and can't be read."* Pinned by `contracts/logic/smartycam-parsers.json` over `contracts/logic/smartycam/*.mov` (`buildSmartyCamMov` in `test/fixtures/build.mjs`) — **a fixture of its own rather than rows in `video-parsers.json`**, whose every file both ports' video contract tests walk; real footage lives in the gitignored `telemetry-samples/aim/`.

- `js/import/mp4.js` — the one MP4 box walker (`bufAt`, `fourcc`, `boxes`, `children`, `child`, and `readMoov`, which skips gigabytes of `mdat` by reading 16-byte headers and reads only the `moov`). `pdr.js`, `pdr25.js`, `gpmf.js` and `mp4cut.js` all read through it; `pdr.js` re-exports `boxes` for its old callers. - `js/import/mp4cut.js` — the browser's best-lap clip cutter (NS-47 ticket 1, `docs/specs/native/NS-47-web-clip-upload.md`): `cutClip(file, window, lapStartS)` is a hand-written **passthrough remuxer** — it reads the source's `moov`, keeps one picture track and at most one AAC track (`pickTracks`, the phones' rule: H.264 / HEVC first, never a JPEG cover image, whether its entry says `jpeg` or an `mp4v` whose esds says so), rebuilds their sample tables for the window (`sampleTable` expands stts / ctts / stsz / stz2 / stsc / stco / co64 / stss into typed arrays) and writes `ftyp` + `moov` + `mdat`, `moov` first. Four rules are load-bearing.

**Zero copy**: the clip is a `Blob` of the new header plus `File.slice()` ranges of the source, so cutting reads only the `moov` and an upload part streams from disk — never `arrayBuffer()` the clip or the source (`mp4cut.test.js` counts the bytes read).

**Determinism**: no clock, no random id, the source's own mvhd / tkhd times — the same file and window give byte-identical output, which is how a closed tab's upload resumes.

**Every time goes through the track's edit list** (`presentation()`): an encoder with B-frames shifts media time behind it, which is the trap that put iOS's first cut 250 ms out.

**Nothing but picture and sound**: no telemetry track, timecode, `udta`, `meta`, `uuid` or `tref`, and both kept tracks are written *enabled* (Android's fixture source disables its H.264 behind a cover image, and AVFoundation won't open a movie with no enabled track). The cut runs from the last sync sample at or before the window to the first at or after its end, exclusive, so `lapOffsetMs` is measured and lands up to a GOP past `PRE_ROLL_S` (3.317 s on a Corvette PDR); sound runs from the frame holding the first kept frame's time and an edit list lines it up. A stts that stops short of its stsz (the Corvette's audio) carries its last duration forward, as ffmpeg does. Refusals are `ClipCutError` with a `reason` (`fragmented`, `no-video`, `no-keyframe`, `window-outside`, `too-large`, `malformed`); `MAX_CLIP_BYTES` mirrors `src/lib/clips.ts` and is pinned to it. Web-only and not ported — the phones have AVFoundation and MediaExtractor — so it has no `contracts/logic/` fixture; it is tested by `test/unit/mp4cut.test.js` over `buildCutSourceMp4` in `test/fixtures/build.mjs` (self-naming synthetic samples, so every clip sample is checked against the source byte for byte) plus an ffmpeg decode of Android's real `clip-source.mp4`, skipped when ffmpeg is absent.

- `js/import/clip-upload.js` — the browser's best-lap clip uploader (NS-47 ticket 2). The map type is remembered in `th-map-type`. The pure half is in `test/unit/map-viewer.test.js`; the DOM half has no fixture and was checked in a browser against a stand-in for MapKit.

- `js/video-sync.js` + `js/lap-stage.js` — video-synced lap analysis, the lap stage (NS-51, `docs/specs/native/NS-51-video-synced-laps.md`). `video-sync.js` is the **pure half, ported and pinned**: the playhead is one fractional grid index `k`, and a clip time maps to it through the lap's `lapTimeSeries` curve and its clip's `lap_offset_ms` (`makeSide`, `gridAtElapsed`, `valueAt`, `sideAtClipTime`, `clipTimeAtGrid`, `scrubRange`); `playheadAt(sides, masterKey, eMaster, mode)` is the whole view — both sides' `{ e, k, distM, inLap, clipMs, inClip, now }`, `deltaS` (A − B at A's place, positive A slower) and in `"time"` mode `gapM` (positive B ahead), **A/B-relative whichever side is the master**; out of the lap both sides keep the master's time from the line, so two cars cross it together. `followRate`, `needsResync` (strictly past `RESYNC_MS`), `matchByFraction` (over `LENGTH_MISMATCH_WARN`), the words (`playheadText`, `cornerPlace`, `fmtStageTime`, `fmtDeltaS`, `fmtGap`, `cornerStops`), `referenceLap` and `stageSides`. Ported as `VideoSync` to the Kit and `:core` under the same names (NS-51 tickets 2 and 3), pinned by `contracts/logic/video-sync.json`; `makeSide` takes the time curve as an optional argument so a stored series can replace the derived one. `lap-stage.js` is the **web-only DOM half**: `bindLapStage(container, opts)` owns up to two `<video>`s with no native controls (the follower muted), the clock (the master's `currentTime`, else a timer — replay), the transport, the now-strip and its own map card, and hands each frame's state to the caller's `onFrame`, which marks its charts; `destroyAllStages()` is called by every view that draws one, since `shell()` re-renders the page. Layout by the stage's **own width** through a `ResizeObserver` (`stageLayout`: wide ≥ 1000 pins the whole stage, medium pins only its transport, compact turns the transport into a fixed mini bar once scrolled past), full-bleed when `(orientation: landscape) and (max-height: 500px)` matches with a video loaded, one module-level `keydown` for the shortcuts, and `currentSyncMode()` / `setSyncMode()` over `th-sync-mode`. Its wrappers (`.lap-stage-box`, `.stage-mount`) are `display: contents` **on purpose**: a sticky element only pins inside its parent, and the stage has to pin over the charts. The charts' side is in `channel-graphs.js`: `showPlayhead(root, mark)` — a solid line, a dot per marked lap from the y-scale every chart now carries (`data-y0` / `data-y1` / `data-padt` / `data-padb` / `data-vscale` / `data-dp`), the value in the title's `.ch-readout`, a ring on the friction circle and balance scatter, the sector table's column lit — **attributes only, never a chart re-render**; `bindChartSeek(root, dStepM, onSeek)` — touch seeks on the tap's click only, so a scroll starting on a chart never moves the video, and a mouse may press and drag; `bindChannelGraphs` gained `onRender` (after every chart render — re-apply the playhead) and `onSelect` (the lit laps changed), and its handle `lit()`, `light(chIdx)`, `slotColor`, `chartsEl`, `details`. `renderTrackMap`'s handle gained `setPlayhead([{ frac, color }])`, which stops the free-running dot while set. `app.js` draws stages on the event page (`stageOpeners`, opened by a lap's ▶ or *Play the lap*; a clip on a lap with no channel entry still opens the plain `showLapVideo` player in the same box), `viewLapCompare` and `viewLeaderboardLap` (their side always replay), through the shared `stageMark` / `stageDeltaFor` / `stageClip` helpers.

- `js/video-wait.js` — the loading mark over a lap video, on the lap player (`showLapVideo`) and every stage slot. A clip is the camera's own file at the camera's bitrate, so the first frame, a seek past what is buffered and a slow connection each leave a black or frozen picture. `waitingAfter(type, { readyState, seeking })` is the pure rule (`test/unit/video-wait.test.js`): `loadstart` / `waiting` / `seeking` wait, a frame or `seeked` / `error` ends it, and **`suspend` with metadata in hand ends the first load**, since with `preload="metadata"` no browser promises `canplay`. `bindVideoWait(video, mark)` shows the first load at once and any later wait only after `WAIT_SHOW_MS` (300), so a buffered seek or the follower being nudged into step never flashes it; it must be bound **before** `src` is set. `srcAt(url, seconds)` in the same module is how both web players **open at their place**: a `#t=` media fragment on the signed URL (the lap player's `playbackStartMs`, the stage slot's current playhead), so the first fetch after the file's index is the place shown rather than the clip's first seconds, with the old seek-on-`loadedmetadata` kept as the backstop; iOS asks its `AVPlayer` for the same place as soon as the player exists (the lap player and `LapStageModel.load`), and Android always did (`setMediaItem(…, startMs)`, a seek before `prepare`). `videoWaitHtml(true)` is also the placeholder while the signed URL is fetched (the lap player draws it in a 16:9 `.video-frame`, so the page doesn't jump). The mark takes no clicks, and under reduced motion the ring stands still and the words *Loading video…* show. The phones' counterparts: iOS's `StageVideo.isBuffering` (KVO on `timeControlStatus`, behind the same 300 ms — every `play()` passes through waiting) drawn by `VideoSlot`, whose accessibility value is `loading` / `buffering` / `ready`, while the lap player is `AVPlayerViewController` and has the system's own; Android's `PlayerView.setShowBuffering` — `ALWAYS` on the lap card's two views, `WHEN_PLAYING` on the stage (a paused scrub seeks per drag event) with a spinner over a stage slot until `StagePlayer.ready`, and a 16:9 spinner box (`lapVideoLoading`) on the lap card while the URL is fetched.

## PWA

- PWA: `manifest.webmanifest` + `sw.js` make the app installable and offline-capable (the service worker covers the app shell and last-known responses; structured offline reads/writes live in `js/offline.js` above). The service worker serves static files stale-while-revalidate (no build step, so no cache busting — changes land on the *second* load), navigations network-first with the cached shell as the offline fallback — cached under `SHELL_URL` (`/login`), never under `/`, and refreshed only by an answer that isn't the landing page (`X-TE-Page: landing`), so a signed-out visit can't make the brochure what a driver gets offline — and `GET /api/*` network-first with cache fallback; `/auth/*` and non-GET are never intercepted. Signing out deletes the cached API data (`th-data-*` caches) so a shared device doesn't retain the previous user's logbook. Icons in `icons/` + `favicon.*` are generated from the brand mark — regenerate rather than hand-edit if the mark changes.
