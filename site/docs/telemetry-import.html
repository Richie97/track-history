Telemetry import

On any event page, or on the New event form, Import video / telemetry… turns recordings into sessions with laps. Files are read on your own device and never leave your computer or phone. The one exception is a best-lap video, a clip of a single lap you can choose to save with Pro.

Importing is free: the lap times, the racing line, top speed, max RPM and max lateral G, and the per-lap speed graph, with throttle and brake where the file carries them. The rest of the channel graphs (steering, RPM, lateral G, gear, the limit marks and the car-health strip), sector splits and lap-vs-lap deltas are part of Track Evolution Pro ($1.99/month or $19.99/year, one subscription across the iPhone, Android and web apps). See Free and Pro.

Import without adding an event first

You don't have to create the event, or name the track, first. Choose Import laps from the + menu (the top bar in the browser, the home screen in the phone apps), or share a file into the phone app. Add the files from one track day or several.

  • Each file is matched to its track by where it was driven. The matching happens on your device.
  • Files are grouped into events by track and date. A two-day weekend becomes one two-day event, and a file from a day you have already logged joins that event.
  • Nothing is saved until you have looked. The review shows one card per event with its track, dates and sessions; Change on a card picks a different track.
  • At a venue with several layouts, the length of your lap decides which one it was. When that can't settle it, the app asks. At a track it knows, a layout it doesn't is offered that track's layouts, or you name the one you drove.
  • A place the app doesn't know asks for a name once and is recognized from then on.

On your phone

The iPhone and Android apps run the same import, with the same lap times, racing line and channel graphs. Tap Import video on an event page and pick the clip from Files or Photos, or share it to Track Evolution (Open with on iPhone, Share on Android). VBO and CSV files import the same way, including the ones Porsche's Track Precision app exports on your phone. On an iPad you can also drag a clip from Files onto an event page, and the same App Store app on an Apple silicon Mac imports from Finder.

The clip is read where it sits. Nothing is copied, so a multi-gigabyte video needs no free space. Saving a best-lap video copies only that lap.

Every lap on the event page opens: tap one for its traces, the gap to the best and, on the session's best lap, the racing line. Compare laps opens the overlay with that lap lit beside the best.

Best-lap video

With Track Evolution Pro, the iPhone, Android and web apps can keep the video of your fastest lap. Importing a Corvette or Cadillac PDR clip or a GoPro clip shows a Save best-lap video switch in the review, on every time. Saving then cuts each session's fastest lap out of the video on your phone or computer (the lap plus a few seconds either side, at the camera's own quality, with sound) and uploads only that clip, with none of the telemetry or location data in the file. A clip is typically 100–150 MB; the switch says roughly how big this import's will be.

A PDR import can keep every lap instead. The switch becomes Save lap videos with a Best lap / Every lap choice, starting on Best lap every time. Every lap saves each full lap of each PDR session as its own clip, from one start/finish crossing to the next, so not the out-lap or in-lap. The switch says how many clips that is and roughly how much space they take; an eight-lap session is typically 700 MB–1 GB. A GoPro clip in the same import still keeps just its best lap.

  • In the browser. The same box sits under the session list. The clip uploads from the open tab: keep the tab open and the card in until the banner at the top says it's done. The session line on the event page shows each clip's progress. Closing the tab stops the upload but keeps the session.
  • On the phones. Clips upload over Wi-Fi unless you turn on Settings → Upload lap videos over cellular; until then the lap says Video waiting for Wi-Fi. The session saves straight away either way, and one saved with no signal uploads its clip once it syncs. An interrupted upload restarts on its own on a good connection. On iPhone the lap shows its progress, offers Retry now after an interruption, and says why if the upload had to be given up. If a clip can't be cut, the session is kept and the lap says so.
  • Playing. Tap a lap marked ▶ on the event page. The video sits above the lap's racing line and runs from a couple of seconds before the start/finish line to a few seconds past the end of the lap. The player's fullscreen button fills the screen; turn a phone sideways for the widest view. In the web app the ▶ opens the lap above the session's charts, with buttons to step to the previous and next lap's video (see Watching a lap). Download in the player's menu saves the file, named after the track, the day and the lap. Playing needs a connection.
  • Who sees it. You; the coaches you've invited, who can watch but never download or delete (the switch says Your coaches can watch it too. when you have any); and anyone with a link to a shared event or session, while you have Pro. A video you upload to YouTube is as public as you set it there. Clips are never on your share page, a leaderboard or what an AI assistant can read.
  • Storage. Each account has 50 GB of lap videos, roughly 400–500 clips, or about 60 sessions saved lap by lap; Settings shows how much you've used. When an import would go over, the switch turns off and says so, and when only saving every lap would, Every lap is grayed out. Nothing is ever deleted to make room.
  • Deleting. Delete video on the lap's page removes it. It also goes when you delete its lap, session or event, or your account. Deleting is always free: if Pro lapses, your clips are kept and locked, and you can still delete them.
  • No video. A phone recording, a VBO log and a CSV export have no video, so their reviews have no switch.

Watching a lap

A lap's video and its charts move together, on the web and in the phone apps. Open a lap from its ▶, or press Play the lap under a session's laps. The video sits above the channel graphs, with a line across every chart at the car's place in the lap. Scrub the bar under the video and the line, the dot on the track map and the read-out beside it (speed, gear, throttle and brake, and the time you're up or down) follow. Tap or click a chart and the video pauses and jumps to that spot.

  • Two laps at once. Light a second lap in the session, or open Compare laps on a track, and both videos play from one button. Same place keeps both cars at the same point on the track, speeding up the slower lap's video. Same time runs both from the line together, so you see one car pull away and by how many feet. Your last choice is remembered.
  • The bar under the video draws where the time went: a climbing line is time lost against the other lap, and the ticks mark the corners and the sector splits.
  • No video? The track map takes the video's place and a dot drives the lap at real speed, with the same line across the charts. Another driver's leaderboard lap always plays this way.
  • On a phone, the other lap's video is a small picture in the corner; tap it to swap which one is big and which one you hear. Turning the phone sideways fills the screen with the video and a read-out over it; turn it back to carry on where you were.
  • Keyboard: Space plays and pauses, ← and → step a second, Option-← and Option-→ about 20 meters, , and . a frame, S switches Same place and Same time, and F fills the screen.

The place on the video comes from the lap's own telemetry, so it can sit a few meters off at times, rarely more than a car length or two.

Upload to YouTube

With Track Evolution Pro, a saved best-lap video can go to your own YouTube channel. Upload to YouTube… is under the lap's video on the event page in the web app, and in the video's menu on the lap's page in the iPhone and Android apps. This is rolling out gradually; if you don't see it yet, it hasn't reached your account.

  • Connecting. The first time, the lap offers Connect YouTube… instead. Google's permission screen opens in your browser, asking only to upload videos to your channel: Track Evolution can't see, change or delete anything already there. It's separate from how you sign in, so it works if you sign in with Apple, and the channel can belong to a different Google account. Settings → YouTube shows which account is connected. Connecting is free.
  • The form. The title and description come filled in with the track, the lap time, the car, the session and the date; you can change both. Visibility is Private, Unlisted or Public, and starts on Unlisted: anyone with the link can watch, but the video isn't in YouTube's search or on your channel's page. The video is marked not made for kids, and no location is sent with it.
  • Uploading. Tap Upload and the clip goes from our storage to YouTube, so you can close the app or the tab, and it uses none of your mobile data. Starting an upload needs a connection. The lap shows Uploading to YouTube · 40%, then On YouTube · Unlisted with an Open link that stays on the lap. If YouTube turns it down, the lap says why and offers Try again.
  • Limits. Each account can upload 10 videos to YouTube a day. YouTube also limits how much the whole app can upload in a day; when that's reached, the lap says Waiting for YouTube — uploads continue tomorrow. and the upload finishes the next day on its own. A lap's video goes up once; after that the lap links to it.
  • Who sees the link. Just you. Coaches never see the YouTube link or the upload controls, and the link isn't on your share page, a leaderboard or what an AI assistant can read. If Pro lapses, the link to a video already on YouTube stays.
  • Deleting. Track Evolution never deletes anything on YouTube; the permission doesn't allow it. Deleting the lap video, its lap, session, event or your account leaves the copy on YouTube, and you delete it in YouTube Studio. If you delete a video on YouTube, its lap offers the upload again within a month.
  • Disconnecting. Settings → YouTube → Disconnect YouTube, free on every plan, withdraws the permission at Google, as does Google's permissions page. Videos you've uploaded stay on YouTube. See the privacy policy for what's stored and sent.

The start/finish line picker

A GoPro clip, a plain .vbo or a phone recording has no lap markers, so the import preview shows the track map you drove. Click where the start/finish line is and laps are timed on each pass across it. These times are accurate to roughly ±0.1–0.3 s and shown with a ~ prefix.

One picked line applies to every file in the batch. The app then remembers it: the next file from that track is timed from the line your last session there used, and you can still tap the map to move it.

How PDR lap times are derived

  • Corvette. The recorder's Beacon events mark start/finish crossings to the millisecond, but it drops some. A missed crossing is worked out from the distance the car had driven, accurate to ~0.05–0.3 s and shown with ~. The method is checked against Cosworth Toolbox lap times. A recording with no beacons gets the line picker. If its GPS can't be read either, it still gets full laps: timed from start/finish when a beacon-timed recording of the same track is in the same batch, otherwise from where the car first reaches pace.
  • Cadillac. The newer recorder in the V-Series Blackwings times laps itself, so its lap times are the recorder's own, to the millisecond. It logs no battery voltage. A clip whose timer didn't run gets the line picker.
Millisecond-exact times are shown as-is. An estimated lap time carries a ~, so you always know which laps are measured and which are estimated.

What an imported session shows

The import preview shows each session's top speed, max RPM and max lateral G. On the event page, the session's stats show its top speed and max G (the highest cornering G of any lap). Each session also stores:

  • Per-lap channel data, sampled along the lap's driven distance: speed for every source, plus, from a PDR recording, RPM, lateral and longitudinal G, throttle, brake, steering angle, yaw rate, gear, wheel slip, boost and whether ABS or traction control was active. A channel the file lacks draws no graph.
  • One figure per lap for each of a PDR recording's slow readings, shown on the Car tab.
  • The session's conditions from a PDR recording: ambient temperature, the track's elevation range and the car's odometer reading. The temperature shows on the session's header and shades the track page's progress chart (see Conditions).

This is worked out at import and the recording is never uploaded, so re-import the file if you delete a session.

The channel graphs

On the event page, the session's lap chips pick the laps for the expandable channel graphs: up to three laps overlaid on one distance axis, in three tabs.

  • Traces: the delta, speed, throttle, brake, steering, RPM and gears stacked on one axis.
  • Grip: the friction circle, balance, lateral G and yaw rate.
  • Car: whether the car is okay and set up right.

Point at a graph, or tap it, to mark that place on every graph, with each selected lap's value in the graph's title and a dot per lap on the track map beside the graphs. A tap or click pins the mark until you clear it.

The Distance / Time switch above the tabs sets the graphs' axis. Distance, the default, lines the laps up corner for corner. Time plots each lap against its own clock, so the laps drift apart by the gap between them and the map's dots show the slower lap falling behind.

Delta and sectors

  • With two or more laps selected, a delta chart above the channels shows where time is gained or lost against the fastest of the selection. Where the trace climbs, you are losing time. See how to find time with a lap delta.
  • A sector table splits each highlighted lap into three sectors along its own driven distance, with no setup, marking the session's best in each sector and the gap to it otherwise.
  • The best sectors summed give a theoretical best lap, which joins the session's stats line when it beats your actual best.

Gears and shift points

  • From a PDR recording, a gear ribbon sits under the RPM trace: one band per selected lap, one block per gear with the number in it, and a gap where the clutch was in. With two or more laps selected, stretches where they were in different gears are outlined.
  • A shift points table gives your upshift RPM from each gear (earliest, typical and latest across the session), with a note when a gear is taken to the top of the day's rev range or shifted out of noticeably earlier than another. See when to shift on track.

ABS, wheelspin and stability marks

A PDR recording's wheel-slip and ABS / traction / stability channels become marks on the best-lap track map: a shape wherever that lap hit ABS, locked a wheel, spun the driven wheels or had a system cut in. Braking-side events are one color and power-side another, and each kind has its own shape. The same stretches are shaded on the brake, throttle and steering traces for every selected lap. The session's stats line counts the places where each happened, or says "no interventions", which is normal on track with the systems off.

Friction circle

On the Grip tab, a PDR recording's cornering and braking/acceleration forces are plotted against each other as a friction circle: one point per 20 m of track for each selected lap, braking at the top, power at the bottom and cornering to the sides, with a dashed arc at the session's strongest combined grip. It is on the phone apps too; see how to read a friction circle.

  • Two numbers under it say how much of your tire work was combined, braking while cornering and cornering while on the power, for each selected lap and for the session.
  • Hover or tap a dot to see where on the lap it happened: the distance is marked on the other graphs and, for the lap the map was drawn from, ringed on the track map.
  • Left and right come from your steering; the recording stores cornering force without a side.
  • 20 m samples smooth the spikes, so the circle shows the shape of your grip use. Your peak G is the max lateral G in the session's stats.

Understeer and oversteer

The balance chart reads understeer and oversteer from the recording's yaw rate and steering angle. It plots each selected lap's samples with steering across and how much the car rotated per metre up, with a dashed line for how this car typically responds. Points above the line are oversteer; below it, understeer.

  • Under the chart, the lap is split into corners: stretches of sustained cornering force in one direction, so a left-right pair counts as two, numbered T1, T2… from the start/finish line (the app's numbering, not the circuit's).
  • Each corner gets a reading per selected lap and for the session, such as "T1 understeer 14%, T7 neutral, T10 slight oversteer 9%".
  • The session's stats show the result in a word (understeer, oversteer, mixed or neutral) with the corners behind it.
  • On the web app, hover a point or a corner row to see where on the lap it is.
  • The reading is relative: each corner is compared with how the car responded across the session at that corner's speed. A car that pushes in every corner, or steadily more as speed rises, reads neutral in every corner; what shows up is the corner that behaves differently. The garage stores your car's wheelbase and steering ratio, but this reading doesn't use them yet.

Balance is on the phone apps too; see understeer and oversteer for what to do about each. The same recordings also let the garage measure your car's steering ratio from your own laps.

The Car tab

The Car tab shows every slow reading a PDR recording carries (oil, coolant and transmission temperature, oil pressure, boost, the four tire pressures and temperatures, fuel and battery voltage) as one small chart per figure across the session's laps. A session typed in by hand shows no Car tab. The tab is on the phone apps too; see engine and brake temperatures on track.

  • Each chart carries the number that matters for it: the peak of a temperature, the lowest oil pressure or voltage, the tire pressure as the lap ended. It is shaded when a figure approaches or passes a line worth watching, such as oil over about 130 °C or the battery under 12.5 V.
  • The tire spread per lap gives left minus right on each axle and front minus rear, for temperatures and pressures.
  • A fuel line gives your burn per lap and the laps left at that rate.
  • On the web a per-lap table carries every figure; on the phones the small charts carry it.
  • The tire pressures feed your setup notebook: the sheet's cold pressures, the hot pressures the import measured and your car's target hot pressure become the cold pressure to start from next time, recordable onto the day's sheet in one tap. On the web this is on the Car tab. In the iPhone and Android apps it sits under the setup notebook on the event page, reading the latest session that recorded tire pressures.

Compare two laps

The track page's Compare two laps puts any two laps with channel data side by side, from any sessions or events. It opens on your latest event's best lap against your all-time best and shows:

  • a head-to-head table: lap time, top/min/average speed, throttle and braking shares;
  • the pair's sector splits and combined theoretical best;
  • the delta chart and the channel graphs on one distance axis.

It's on the phone apps as well as the web, and works offline for laps you've viewed before. If the two laps cover clearly different driven distances (a different layout, or a different start/finish line between imports), the page says so.
