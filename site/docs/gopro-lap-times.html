How to get lap times from GoPro video

A GoPro with GPS records where the camera was alongside the picture, which is enough to time laps. Track Evolution reads that GPS straight from the video file, shows you the track you drove, and times a lap each time the car crosses the start/finish line you pick. The video stays on your computer or phone.

You pick the line once per track. The next import from a track you have already timed uses the line your last session there used, and the map is still there if you want to move it.

What you need

  • A GoPro that records GPS, HERO5 or newer. Not every model has it (the HERO12 Black has none), so check yours.
  • GPS switched on in the camera's settings before you record. Footage shot with it off has no positions in it and can't be timed afterwards.
  • The camera's original files (.mp4). A video that was edited, re-encoded or downloaded from a sharing site has lost its GPS.
  • A free Track Evolution account, on the web at trackevolution.app or in the iPhone or Android app.

Turn the camera on a few minutes before you go out, so it has a GPS position by the time you're on track.

In the browser

  1. Put the camera's SD card in your computer, or copy the videos off it.
  2. Open the event at trackevolution.app, or start a New event.
  3. Press Import video / telemetry… and choose the videos, or drag them onto the box. Each file becomes its own session.
  4. The review draws the track from the camera's GPS. Click where the start/finish line is. One click covers every file in the import.
  5. Check the laps, then save.

On your phone

  1. Bring the clips over from the camera as you normally would. They land in Photos or Files.
  2. Open the event in the Track Evolution app and tap Import video, then pick the clips. Or share a clip to Track Evolution (Open with on iPhone, Share on Android).
  3. Tap the start/finish line on the map, check the laps, and save.

The video is read where it sits. Nothing is copied, so a long session needs no extra free space on the phone.

How accurate the lap times are

A GoPro records its position 10 to 18 times a second, and a lap is timed between the positions either side of the line. That puts each lap within roughly 0.1 to 0.3 seconds, which is why the times carry a ~. It is plenty for seeing whether you got faster and how consistent you were. Phone GPS versus a dedicated lap timer covers what that precision is and isn't good for.

What you get

  • Lap times for every full lap, and the session's best.
  • Your racing line for the best lap, drawn as a track map.
  • A speed graph for every lap, laid over each other by distance around the track.

A GoPro isn't connected to the car, so there is no throttle, brake, RPM or steering in the footage.

What is free and what is Pro

Importing is free, with no limit on files: the lap times, the racing line and the speed graph for every lap. Track Evolution Pro ($1.99 a month or $19.99 a year, one subscription across the web, iPhone and Android) adds sector splits and a theoretical best lap, lap-against-lap time deltas, and best-lap video: your fastest lap cut out of the footage and kept with the session, playing in step with its graphs.

If it doesn't work

  • "No GPS data in this video (was GPS enabled on the camera?)". The camera recorded no positions. Either GPS was off, the model has none, or it never found a position. That footage can't be timed; turn GPS on for next time, and type this session's laps in by hand if you have them.
  • "No PDR, SmartyCam or GoPro telemetry in this video". The file isn't the camera's original. Use the one from the SD card.
  • "No laps cross the picked line". Click a different spot, on a part of the track the car drove on every lap.
  • The first lap is missing. A lap needs a crossing at each end, so the out-lap and the lap you pitted on aren't counted.
