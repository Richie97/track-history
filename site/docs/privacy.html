Privacy policy

Track Evolution (trackevolution.app) is a personal track-day logbook operated by Speedshift LLC — free to use, with an optional Track Evolution Pro subscription. This policy explains, in plain language, what data the app collects and what happens to it. Effective October 17, 2026.

The short version: your logbook is private to your account, there are no ads, and your data is never sold. The website and the iPhone and Android apps count visits and screen views with Google Analytics, which never sees what's in your logbook. Video and telemetry files are read on your own device and never uploaded — the one exception is a best-lap video you choose to save, which is only that lap. We never see your payment details. The only thing ever made public is a share page or a shared event or session you explicitly turn on, or a best-lap video you upload to your own YouTube channel. Nothing reaches an AI assistant unless you connect one yourself, and nobody else reads your logbook unless you invite them as your coach.

What we collect

  • Sign-in details. Signing in with Google gives us your Google account ID, email address, name, and profile-picture URL — the standard openid email profile scopes and nothing more. Signing in with Apple gives us your Apple account ID, your name (only on the first sign-in), and your email address — which can be Apple's private relay address if you choose Hide My Email. We never see your Google or Apple password and can't access anything else in those accounts.
  • What you log. The tracks, events, sessions, lap times, notes, goals, car and conditions details you enter, what your track days cost if you choose to record it, the vehicles in your garage — including the consumable parts, costs, wear measurements and per-day setup sheets you record for them — any share slug you choose, your settings (the prep-checklist list you start from and your choice of metric or imperial units), and your two leaderboard choices: whether you appear on per-track leaderboards, and whether other ranked drivers may open your ranked lap. This is stored so the app can show it back to you. Garage, setup and cost data is never included in share pages or leaderboards.
  • Your driver profile, if you fill it in. Your occupation, driving experience, competition licence, instruction so far, safety gear, what you want to work on, and anything you tell your instructor in the free-text box. Every field is optional. If you choose to mention a medical condition or an injury there, it is stored like any other text you write. The profile has no birth date or emergency contact. It is shown only to you and to coaches you invite.
  • Coaching connections. If you invite a coach, or accept an invitation, we store who can read whose logbook, when that started and when the coach last looked. An invite link's code is stored only in a scrambled (hashed) form and stops working when it is used or after seven days.
  • A YouTube connection, if you make one. If you connect YouTube to upload best-lap videos, we store the permission Google grants, encrypted, the connected account's email address, the channel's name, and a record of each upload — see YouTube below.
  • Location, only while recording laps. The mobile app's lap recorder uses your phone's GPS during a recording you start yourself — see location data below for exactly what stays on the phone and what is saved.
  • Cookies, or a sign-in token. On the web, a session cookie keeps you signed in and a short-lived cookie protects the sign-in flow itself. The iPhone and Android apps use a sign-in token held in the operating system's encrypted storage instead. On the website, Google Analytics also sets its own cookies to count visits, if you allow it, and in the apps it keeps a random identifier for the installation — see analytics below. There are no advertising cookies or advertising identifiers.
  • Subscription state. If you subscribe to Track Evolution Pro, the App Store or Google Play tells us that a purchase was made and we store the store's own transaction identifier for it, which product and plan it is, whether it is active, when it renews or expires, and whether auto-renew is on. That is what lets one subscription work across the web, iPhone and Android. We never receive or store your card, bank or payment details — the store handles payment entirely, and the identifier we keep is meaningless outside it.
  • On your device only. Your theme preference, and a cache of pages and recently viewed logbook data so things work offline. Changes you make offline are also held on the device until they can be sent, and a lap recording stays there until you save or discard it. None of this leaves your device on its own.

Telemetry and video files

Telemetry import — Corvette and Cadillac PDR and GoPro video, Racelogic VBO, Porsche Track Precision CSV — runs entirely on your own device, in your browser or in the iPhone and Android apps. Videos are read by byte range and files are never uploaded to our servers. The only thing stored is what you choose to save at the end of an import: the resulting sessions and lap times, plus the telemetry-derived extras the app shows — the best lap's GPS racing line and per-lap channel data (speed, RPM, lateral G) — as ordinary logbook entries. The recordings themselves are never stored.

Best-lap videos (Pro). When you import a video in the iPhone or Android app or in your browser with Track Evolution Pro, you can choose Save best-lap video. Then a clip of each imported session's fastest lap — a few seconds either side of it, with its sound — is cut on your device and uploaded to our cloud storage. Only that lap is uploaded, never the rest of the video, and the clip carries no telemetry track and no location metadata. You can turn the switch off for any import, and nothing is uploaded. A saved clip is visible to you and to the coaches you've invited, and never appears on your share page, a leaderboard or to an AI assistant you connect. You can also upload it to your own YouTube channel — see YouTube. It is deleted when you delete it, its lap, session or event, or your account; a copy you uploaded to YouTube stays there. Removing a coach stops them watching your videos, but it can't take back a video they already saved.

Importing a video in the iPhone app from your photo library asks for permission to read it. That access is used for one thing: opening the clip you picked so its telemetry track can be read where it sits. The video is not copied, not moved and not uploaded — unless you choose to save its best-lap video, when only that lap is copied and uploaded — nothing else in your library is read, and you can revoke the permission at any time in your phone's settings. Importing from Files needs no permission at all.

Location data (recording laps in the mobile app)

The iPhone/Android app can record a track session's laps from your phone's GPS. Location is collected only while you are actively recording — you start and stop it yourself — and, because the phone is usually stowed while driving, recording continues while the app is in the background. The GPS trace is kept on your phone while you record and until you save or discard it. Saving uploads only the derived results — lap times, the best lap's racing line, and per-lap speed — as ordinary logbook entries, the same as a telemetry import; the fix-by-fix recording itself is never uploaded and is deleted from the phone once saved or discarded. The app never collects your location outside of a recording, and you can revoke location permission at any time in your phone's settings.

Analytics

The web app at trackevolution.app and this documentation site use Google Analytics to count visits, so we can see which pages people use and where they came from. Google receives what any website's visit counter does: which page was viewed, the referring site, your browser, device type and screen size, your approximate location (derived from your IP address by Google), and a random identifier kept in Google Analytics cookies so repeat visits can be counted. Both sites also load Google Tag Manager, Google's tool for managing these measurement tags; it loads only when Google Analytics does, under the same choice described below, and sets no cookies of its own.

  • You choose. In the UK and the EU — recognised by your device's time zone being a European one — the website asks first, and neither Google Analytics nor Google Tag Manager loads at all unless you accept. If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland but your device's clock says otherwise, Google is told not to store analytics cookies for you; it may still receive an anonymous page view, with no cookie and no identifier. Everywhere else, Google Analytics runs unless you decline.
  • Change your mind at any time with Cookies in the footer, or Settings → Cookie settings in the web app. Declining stops Google Analytics and deletes its cookies. Your choice is remembered in your browser — separately for the web app and this documentation site — and nowhere else.
  • In the web app, a page is reported only by its kind — "an event page", "a track's leaderboard" — never with the record it shows, the name or slug of a share page, or anything you have entered. Your logbook, email address, name and account are never sent to Google Analytics.
  • The iPhone and Android apps use Google Analytics for Firebase the same way: they report which kind of screen you open — "an event page", "the lap recorder" — and never the record it shows, a share page's name, or anything you have entered. Google also receives your device model, operating system and app version, your approximate location (derived from your IP address by Google), and a random identifier for the installation, which resets if you reinstall the app. The apps do not collect your phone's advertising identifier, do not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and send no data to Google Analytics for advertising.
  • Google handles this data under Google's privacy policy; see how Google uses information from sites that use its services.
  • On the website, besides declining, Google's Analytics opt-out browser add-on, blocking cookies for these sites, or a content blocker all work too — the app works exactly the same without it.

What we don't do

  • No advertising and no advertising trackers. The one third-party analytics service, on the website and in the apps, is Google Analytics.
  • We never sell or rent your data.
  • We don't share your data with anyone, except the cloud hosting provider that stores it so the service can run, Google for the website's and the apps' visit counts, an AI assistant you connect yourself, a video you upload to YouTube yourself, or if the law requires it.
  • We never see, handle or store payment details — Apple and Google take payment and tell us only whether a subscription is active.

Who can see your logbook

Your logbook is private to your account. There are a few exceptions, each of which you turn on yourself and can turn off at any time:

  • A share page, which you create under a slug you choose: it shows stats and best times but strips notes, your email, and per-lap data. Pasting its link into a chat produces a preview built from the same public stats.
  • A link to one event or one session, which you create from that event or session: anyone with the link can see that item's lap times, racing line, recorded conditions, telemetry and best-lap video, with your name. It never includes your notes, checklist, costs, setup sheets or garage, and nothing else in your logbook. Stopping sharing, or deleting the item, turns the link off.
  • Per-track leaderboards, which are off by default: opting in shows your display name and your best device-timed lap per track (with its date) to other signed-in users — only laps recorded with the app or imported from telemetry are ranked, never a time you typed in. Nothing else — no notes, laps, garage or setup data — is included, and opting out removes you from every leaderboard immediately.
  • Your ranked lap itself, if you separately turn on Let other drivers open my ranked laps. This is a second choice, off unless you make it, and it publishes one lap per track — the ranked one already on the board — as its racing line and its telemetry traces, so a driver ranked at the same track can compare corner for corner. It never publishes another lap, the session it came from, your notes, session labels, car, typed conditions, setup sheets or garage. Turning it off, or leaving the leaderboards, closes those laps immediately.
  • Coaches you invite (Pro). An invite link you send lets the person who opens it read your logbook — your events, sessions, laps and telemetry, racing lines and conditions, your best-lap videos (to watch, not to download), your cars and their modifications, and your driver profile — along with your name and profile picture. They can't change anything, and they never see your email, notes, prep checklists, costs, setup sheets or garage parts. Treat the link like a key: whoever opens it first gets that access, so send it only to your coach. You can see who has access and remove them at any time from the Coaching page, and a coach who is removed loses access immediately — but anything they already saw or wrote down is theirs, the same as a printout would be. That includes a video: a link to play one stays valid for up to an hour, and any player that can play it can also save it. When you accept an invite yourself, the driver who sent it sees your name and profile picture.
  • A best-lap video you upload to YouTube. Who can watch it there is the visibility you choose — Private, Unlisted or Public — and YouTube's own settings. See YouTube below.

AI assistants you connect

With Track Evolution Pro you can connect an AI assistant — Claude, ChatGPT or another app that supports connectors — to your logbook (see AI assistants). This is off unless you set it up, and only you can set it up: the assistant sends you to Track Evolution to sign in and approve it.

  • Once connected, when you ask the assistant something it can read the parts of your logbook it needs to answer — tracks, events, sessions, lap times and telemetry, your garage, setup sheets and notes, and the leaderboards you can already see. It can't change or delete anything.
  • What the assistant reads is sent to that assistant's provider and is handled under their terms and privacy policy and your account with them, not ours.
  • We keep a record of each connection — the app's name, when you connected it and when it was last used — so you can see and remove it, plus the credentials the assistant uses, stored in a form that can't be used if our database were ever exposed.
  • You can disconnect an assistant at any time under Settings → AI assistants in the web app; it loses access immediately. Its access also ends on its own if it goes unused for 90 days, and pauses while you don't have Pro.

YouTube

With Track Evolution Pro you can upload a saved best-lap video to your own YouTube channel (see Upload to YouTube). This is off unless you set it up, and every upload is one you start yourself. Track Evolution uses YouTube API Services to do it. Uploading means you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google handles the video and your YouTube account under the Google Privacy Policy.

  • Connecting. Connect YouTube opens Google's permission screen in your browser. It asks for permission to upload videos to your channel, and for that Google account's email address so Settings can show which account is connected — nothing more. Track Evolution can't see, change or delete anything on your channel. This is a separate permission from signing in, so it works if you sign in with Apple, and the channel can belong to a different Google account from the one you sign in with.
  • What we store. The permission Google grants, stored encrypted; the connected account's email address; and the channel's name, once the first upload reports it. For each upload: the title, description and visibility you approved, how far the upload has got, and the YouTube video's ID, so the lap can link to it.
  • What we send YouTube. Only the clip itself and the title, description and visibility you approved, plus the category Autos & Vehicles, a few tags (track day, HPDE, the track's name and your car) and that the video is not made for kids. No location is ever sent, and the clip carries none. The upload goes from our server to YouTube, not from your phone.
  • A transfer you start. An upload is a transfer you start yourself, of a video you picked, to a service you sign in to yourself. We never upload anything on our own, and nothing else from your logbook goes to YouTube. Once a video is there, YouTube holds it under its own terms, not ours.
  • Who sees the link in the app. Only you. Coaches you invite never see it, and it isn't on your share page, a leaderboard or what an AI assistant you connect can read.
  • Kept up to date. The YouTube data we keep is re-checked with Google, or deleted, at least every 30 days. The permission is checked; a video that is no longer on YouTube has its record deleted, and its lap offers the upload again; and if the permission has been removed, the data we hold about the connection is deleted.
  • Disconnecting. Settings → YouTube → Disconnect YouTube, on any plan, withdraws the permission at Google and deletes the connection. You can also remove Track Evolution's access yourself on Google's permissions page. Deleting your Track Evolution account withdraws the permission too, within a day.
  • We never delete a YouTube video, and the permission doesn't allow it. Disconnecting leaves the videos you've uploaded on YouTube. Deleting the clip in the app, or its lap, session or event, or your account, deletes our record of the upload and leaves the copy on YouTube; delete it in YouTube Studio.

Where your data lives

Your logbook is stored in a database run by our cloud hosting provider, and best-lap videos in the same provider's file storage, private to the service — a video is only reachable through a link the app asks for, which stops working after an hour. Both are accessed only to operate the service.

Retention and deletion

You can download a copy of your data at any time from Settings → Your data on the web: your laps and events as spreadsheet files, and everything — tracks, events, sessions, laps, telemetry, setup sheets, your garage, your profile and preferences, and who you've given access to — as one file, which lists your best-lap videos; each video downloads from its lap's page. The copy is complete whatever your plan, including data only Track Evolution Pro shows in the app. It never contains sign-in credentials or store payment identifiers.

Your data is kept for as long as you use the service. You can delete individual tracks, events, sessions and laps in the app at any time. To have your account and all of its data deleted, follow the steps on Delete your account — in short, email eric@speedshift.io from the address you sign in with. You can also revoke Track Evolution's access to your Google account from your Google account settings, or to your Apple account from account.apple.com under Sign in with Apple. A YouTube connection is a separate Google permission, removed on Google's permissions page or in Settings.

Deleting your account also ends every coaching connection it is part of, in either direction, and deletes your driver profile and your best-lap videos — the video files themselves are removed from storage within a day. If you connected YouTube, the permission is withdrawn at Google within a day and our records of your uploads are deleted; videos already on YouTube stay there, and you delete those in YouTube Studio.

Deleting your account deletes the subscription record we hold alongside the rest of your data. It does not cancel the subscription itself, which lives with the App Store or Google Play — cancel that separately, before you ask for deletion.

Children

Track Evolution is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect data from them.
