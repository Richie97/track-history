Data model & stats

Your logbook mirrors the shape of a season: tracks → events → sessions → laps. Every stat is derived from that, by the rules below.

The hierarchy

  • Track: identified by name, matched case-insensitively. Put the layout in the name ("Virginia International Raceway (Full)" vs. "(Patriot)"): a different layout is a different track, so best times and goals never mix.
  • Event: a day (or weekend) at a track, with notes and an optional manual best time. A future event shows in the dashboard's upcoming section with a countdown and prep checklist, and counts toward your totals and per-track stats once its start date arrives.
  • Session: one run group or stint within an event, with an optional name and its own notes. Imported sessions can also carry the best lap's racing line and per-lap channel data (speed, RPM, lateral G), shown as the channel graphs on the event page.
  • Lap: a single lap time, entered and shown as m:ss.fff and stored to the millisecond.

A session saved with a GPS trace remembers where its racing line is. Open on a map (web, iPhone, iPad and Android) lays it over satellite imagery of the circuit: drag, pinch and rotate, and tap or click the line to read the speed there. An older session with no saved position borrows one from a newer session of yours at the same track, and the map says when a position is estimated. With nothing to borrow from, the line opens on its own.

Prep checklist

A future event carries a prep checklist. Tick items off as you pack, and the dashboard's countdown card shows how far through you are.

New checklists start from your own list, which begins as the app's default. Open Settings (the account button) → Prep checklist to add, remove and reorder items. The list is saved to your account, so the phone and the browser offer the same one. Editing it never changes a checklist already started on an event. For what belongs on it, see the track-day prep checklist.

Best time

An event's best time is MIN(best logged lap, manual best). The manual field is for events where you only recorded a single best time; you can attach full lap data later. Imported per-session bests appear as one-lap sessions.

Consistency

Consistency is the coefficient of variation (standard deviation ÷ mean) of an event's clean laps: those within 7% of their own session's best. It is shown once an event has three of them, and lower is more consistent. The rule leaves out out-laps, cool-down laps and laps lost in heavy traffic, and judges a wet session against its own best. Every lap still counts toward your lap count and on-track hours.

Per-session analysis also derives best-N averages, a pace slope over the stint, and warmup-lap detection. For how to use the number, see lap time consistency and your theoretical best.

Sectors and the theoretical best

Sessions with telemetry (an imported video or logger file, or laps recorded with the phone) get sector splits with no setup. Each lap is cut into three equal slices of its own driven distance, and its three splits add up to exactly its lap time. Because sectors are fractions of each lap's own length, the same corner stays in the same sector when laps differ a little in driven distance.

The session's best time in each sector, summed, is the theoretical best lap, shown beside your actual best when it's quicker.

Conditions

The track page's best lap per event chart carries a faint shaded band behind it: one panel per event, deepening with the air temperature, with a key underneath. Each session's header shows the temperature that session was driven in, and the track's elevation change sits on its summary line.

  • Where a video import recorded the outside air temperature, that is what you see, as a range when the day warmed up between sessions. Otherwise the temperature you typed on the event is used. The two never overwrite each other.
  • An event with no temperature isn't shaded.
  • The band is left off when there is nothing to compare, such as two events at much the same temperature.

On-track hours

Each event carries an on-track hours figure: the total of its logged lap times once it has three or more laps, otherwise an hour and a quarter per track day. You can override it on the event's edit form. It doesn't affect any lap stats; it drives the garage's consumable wear tracking. See Garage & setup notebook.

What a day cost

An event can carry what it cost: entry fee, fuel, travel & lodging and misc, each optional, entered in dollars on the event's form (on the phones, behind What it cost). A blank line is unknown, not zero: an event with nothing entered drops out of every roll-up. The event page shows the total with its breakdown.

Entering costs is free. Rolling them up is part of Track Evolution Pro and counts past events only:

  • The track page says what that track has cost you.
  • A car's garage page adds its track days to what its parts cost.
  • Year in review totals the season by line item. For every track where the year's best beat every year before, it gives the cost per second found and calls out the year's cheapest seconds. A track-year that got slower has no price per second.

Costs are private: they never appear on your share page or a leaderboard. For what to budget for, see what a track day costs.

Share pages

You can publish your logbook read-only under a slug you choose (trackevolution.app/share/<your-slug>).

  • Shows: stats and times.
  • Never shows: notes, your email, what your days cost, or per-lap data.
  • Its link preview carries your name, event count and headline bests: only what the page itself shows.

Once you have a share page, each season's Wrapped gets one too, at trackevolution.app/share/<your-slug>/wrapped/2026.

  • Shows: the same story you see (track days, laps, most driven, fastest lap, the time you found, the hottest day), all of it already on your share page. Track miles are your laps times each track's length.
  • Never shows: the Pro cards. No favorite tire, no top speed, nothing from your garage or your telemetry.
  • Its link preview shows your season's headline numbers, and turning off your share page turns it off too.

Sharing one day or one session

Separately from your share page, any event or session can have a link of its own (trackevolution.app/share/e/… for a day, /share/s/… for a session). It opens without signing in and shows that item alone.

  • Shows: the lap times, the best-lap racing line, the recorded conditions, the telemetry and the best-lap video.
  • Never shows: your notes, the prep checklist, what the day cost, your setup sheets or your garage.
  • A session's link shows only that session. Its best lap, lap count and consistency are worked out from it, not from the rest of the day.
  • What the telemetry shows follows your plan, not the viewer's: with Pro, the whole analysis panel and your best-lap video; on the free plan, the speed, throttle and brake traces and no video.
  • Each item has one link; sharing it again gives the same link. Stop sharing turns it off at once, and so does deleting the event or session.

A single lap can have a link as well (/share/l/…), and so can two laps compared (/share/c/…).

  • A lap's link shows that lap and nothing else of its session: its time, its telemetry, its video, and the racing line when it is the session's best lap.
  • A comparison shows the two laps head to head, with the time delta, sector splits and traces side by side. The laps can be from different days at the same track, and nothing else from either day comes with them.
  • The comparison is a Pro view, so on the free plan the link shows the two lap times.

Coaches

A coach you invite reads your logbook signed in as themselves, never as you, and only ever reads it.

  • They see: your events, sessions, laps and telemetry (the channel panel as far as your plan includes it, whatever their own plan is), your racing lines and conditions, your cars and their modifications, your driver profile, and your name and picture.
  • They never see: your email, notes, prep checklists, what your days cost, setup sheets, garage parts or wear, your leaderboards or your Wrapped.
  • Removing a coach ends their access on their next page load, and deleting either account ends it too.

A coach can leave comments on a session, on a lap, or at a marked place on a lap, and you can reply.

  • Each coach has their own conversation with you. One coach never sees another's comments, or that you have another coach.
  • Comments are not part of your share pages or shared links, and an AI assistant you connect doesn't read them.
  • You can delete any comment in your logbook; a coach can edit or delete only their own.
  • If you remove a coach, their comments stay with you and the conversation closes. Deleting either account deletes the comments that account wrote or received.

Leaderboards

Every track in the app's catalog has a leaderboard of drivers' best laps, behind the Leaderboard button on the track page. You can see it before you join, and before your first event there. Leaderboards never mix layouts.

  • Strictly opt-in. Join in Settings → Leaderboards, or with the join button on the leaderboard.
  • Joining shares exactly two things per track: your display name and your best device-timed lap, with its date.
  • Stays private: your events, notes, laps, garage and setup sheets.
  • Leaving removes you from every leaderboard immediately.

Only laps the app timed are ranked: laps recorded with the phone's GPS or imported from telemetry (GoPro, Corvette or Cadillac PDR, AiM SmartyCam, VBO, Track Precision CSV). Times you type in, and the manual "best time" on an event, count toward your own bests and progress chart but never reach a leaderboard, so your logbook best can be faster than your leaderboard row. The leaderboard says so.

Opening a lap is a second, separate choice, off unless you turn it on (Settings → Leaderboards → Let other drivers open my ranked laps, or the button on the leaderboard).

  • With it on, a driver ranked at the same track can tap your row and see that lap (its racing line and its speed, throttle, brake and steering traces) next to their own best there.
  • It publishes one lap per track, the ranked one already on the board. Never another lap, the session it came from, or your notes, session labels, car, conditions, setup sheets or garage.
  • Leaving the leaderboards turns it off, and turning it off closes those laps straight away.
  • Seeing another driver's traces needs Pro; their line and their times don't.

Each track also has a public page at trackevolution.app/tracks, readable without an account.

  • Shows: the track's length, how many drivers are on its leaderboard, and their best laps as times alone.
  • Never shows: a name, a date or a car, so nobody can tell whose a time is.
  • Leaving the leaderboards takes your time off it.

Privacy posture

  • Your logbook is private to your account (Google or Apple sign-in). Every account gets its own separate, empty logbook.
  • Telemetry and video are read on your device and never uploaded, except a lap video you choose to save with Pro (the best lap, or every lap of a PDR session): only those laps, visible to you, the coaches you invite, and anyone you send a shared event, session or lap link. A video you upload to YouTube is as public as you set it there.
  • Share pages are opt-in, strip notes, your email and per-lap data, and never carry a best-lap video.
  • Leaderboards are opt-in and carry only your name and best lap per track. The public track pages show those best laps as times, with no name.

What's collected, retention and deletion are in the privacy policy.
