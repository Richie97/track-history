Data model & stats

Your logbook mirrors the shape of a season: tracks → events → sessions → laps. Everything below is derived from that, with a couple of deliberate rules worth knowing.

The hierarchy

  • Track — identified by name, matched case-insensitively. Put the layout in the name (e.g. "Virginia International Raceway (Full)" vs. "(Patriot)") — a layout change is a different track for record-keeping, so best times and goals never mix across layouts.
  • Event — a day (or weekend) at a track, with notes and an optional manual best time. An event dated in the future shows in the dashboard's upcoming section with a countdown and prep checklist; it only starts counting toward your event/track-day totals and per-track stats once its start date arrives.
  • Session — one run group / stint within an event. Imported sessions can also carry the best lap's racing line and per-lap channel data (speed, RPM, lateral G), shown as the channel graphs on the event page.
  • Lap — a single lap time, entered and shown as m:ss.fff and stored to the millisecond.

Prep checklist

An event dated in the future carries a prep checklist — tick items off as you pack, and the dashboard's countdown card shows how far through you are.

The list a new checklist starts from is yours. Open Settings (the account button) → Prep checklist to add, remove and reorder items; the app ships a default to start you off, and your first edit makes the list your own. It is saved to your account, so the phone and the browser offer the same list.

Editing that list never changes a checklist you have already started on an event. Those are a copy taken at the time, so nothing you have already ticked off gets untidied by a later edit.

Best time

An event's best time is MIN(best logged lap, manual best). The manual field exists because spreadsheet-era events often only recorded a single best time — you can import those as-is and attach full lap data later, either via the seed data or pasted into the UI. Imported per-session bests appear as one-lap sessions.

Consistency

Consistency is the coefficient of variation (standard deviation ÷ mean) of all laps in an event, shown once an event has 3+ laps. Lower is more consistent. Per-session analysis also derives best-N averages, a pace slope over the stint, and warmup-lap detection.

Sectors and the theoretical best

Sessions with telemetry (an imported video or logger file, or laps recorded with the phone) get sector splits with no setup: each lap is cut into three equal slices of its own driven distance and the time in each slice is read off the lap's speed trace, so every lap's three splits add up to exactly its lap time. The best of each sector across the session summed together is the theoretical best lap — shown beside your actual best when it's quicker. The gap between the two is what stringing your best sectors into one lap would have been worth. Sectors are fractions of each lap's own length rather than fixed points on the track, which keeps the same corner in the same sector even when laps differ a little in driven distance.

On-track hours

Each event also carries an on-track hours figure — 2 hours per track day by default, overridable on the event's edit form. It doesn't affect any lap stats; it's the wear clock behind the garage's consumable tracking. See Garage & setup notebook.

Share pages

You can publish your logbook read-only under a slug you choose (trackevolution.app/share/<your-slug>). The share page shows stats and times but strips notes, your email, and per-lap data — it's the only part of your logbook visible without signing in. Pasting the link into a chat previews with your name, event count and headline bests; the preview uses only what the share page itself shows.

Leaderboards

Every track the app's catalog knows has a per-track leaderboard of Track Evolution drivers' best laps, visible to signed-in users on the track page. It is strictly opt-in (Settings → Leaderboards, or the track page's join button), and joining shares exactly two things per track: your display name and your best lap, with its date. Your events, notes, laps, garage and setup sheets stay private, and leaving removes you from every leaderboard immediately. Leaderboards never mix layouts — the full course and a short configuration are separate tracks.

Privacy posture

  • Your logbook is private to your account (Google or Apple sign-in) — every account gets its own separate, empty logbook.
  • Telemetry and video parsing is entirely client-side — recordings are never uploaded.
  • Share pages are opt-in and strip notes, your email, and per-lap data.
  • Leaderboards are opt-in and carry only your name and best lap per track.

The full details — what's collected, retention, deletion — are in the privacy policy.
