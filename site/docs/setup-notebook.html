Keeping a setup notebook

Six months from now you will be back at the same circuit, in the same car, wondering what tire pressures you ran and whether that alignment change helped. A setup notebook is the answer to both. It takes two minutes a day and it is the difference between developing a car and guessing at one.

What it is for

  • A baseline to return to. When a change makes the car worse, you need to know exactly what "before" was.
  • A starting point for next time. The pressures that worked at this track in this weather are worth more than any general advice.
  • Evidence. Memory keeps the dramatic moments and loses the numbers. "It felt better" is not the same as "it was three tenths quicker and the fronts wore evenly".

What to write down

One entry per day at the track:

SectionWhat to record
The day Date, circuit and layout, weather and air temperature, dry or wet
Tires Which set, cold pressures as set, hot pressures off the track, all four corners
Alignment Camber, toe and caster, front and rear, and when it was last measured
Suspension Damper settings in clicks, anti-roll bar positions, ride height if adjustable
Brakes Which pads, and anything about the pedal
Fuel How much you started with. Fuel load changes the car's weight and balance.
The result Best lap, how consistent the laps were, and a sentence on how the car felt, by corner if you can

Write the feel down the moment you get out of the car. By the evening it has become a general impression.

One change at a time

  1. Start from a known baseline and run a session on it.
  2. Decide what the car is doing wrong, and where. "Pushes in the middle of slow corners" is something you can act on; "feels slow" is not. See understeer and oversteer.
  3. Change one thing, by a small step.
  4. Run a session and write down what happened, including the lap times.
  5. Keep it or put it back before trying the next.

Two changes at once tell you nothing about either. It is tempting on a day with only four sessions, and it is how people end up with a car they cannot explain.

Judging a change honestly

Lap times move for reasons that have nothing to do with the setup. The track gets quicker as rubber goes down and slower in the afternoon heat, the tires age through the day, traffic comes and goes, and you improve as you learn the circuit. A change that coincides with a faster lap did not necessarily cause it.

  • Compare several clean laps before and after, not one best lap against another.
  • Look at tire wear and pressures as well as the stopwatch.
  • If a change matters, test it again on another day.
  • Trust a result more when the car also feels the way you expected.

The setup notebook in Track Evolution

In Track Evolution every event carries one setup sheet per day: tire pressures cold and hot for all four corners, camber, toe, caster, damper clicks, anti-roll bar settings, fuel, and which pad and tire sets were on the car. A new sheet starts as a copy of your previous day's, so you only enter what changed, and the values that differ from the day before are highlighted. It works on the web and in the phone apps, and without signal: a sheet filled in at the car saves and syncs once you are back online.

Because the sheet sits on the event, the result is already recorded beside it: the day's best lap and consistency. On the web, each track's page ends with a Setup vs. lap times table — every sheet you have logged there in date order, what changed between them, and the lap time alongside. The app does the remembering and leaves the conclusion to you, for the reasons above.

When an imported recording carries tire pressures, the notebook also suggests the cold pressure to start from next time.

The setup notebook is part of Track Evolution Pro, and setup sheets are private: they never appear on a share page, and a coach you share your logbook with does not see them.
