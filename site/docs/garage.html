Garage & setup notebook

Track what's on the car and what it's set to. Your logged events then tell you when consumables will need replacing and what a setup change did to your times.

Keeping your cars is free: every vehicle, its notes, the default that fills in new events, and each car's track days and best laps. Consumable wear tracking, what the car has cost and the setup notebook are part of Track Evolution Pro ($1.99/month or $19.99/year, one subscription across the iPhone, Android and web apps). See Free and Pro.

Vehicles and their garage pages

Open the garage from the Garage link beside Events at the top of every page on the web, or the Garage tab in the iPhone, iPad and Android apps. It shows a card for each car, with its track days and where it was last out. + Add car adds one, picked from the catalog or typed in.

When an event's Car field matches a garage vehicle by name, the event links to that vehicle. That link is what lets parts accrue wear and setups carry over between events.

Each car's page shows:

  • its track days, where it was last out and where it goes next;
  • Best laps, its best lap at each track;
  • Edit car, for its name, modifications, notes and target hot tire pressure (the pressure you want the tires to reach on track, which the pressure loop aims at and an imported session's Car tab can also set);
  • with Pro, its track hours, consumables and spend.

Wheelbase and steering ratio

Edit car takes two optional numbers from the car's spec sheet: its wheelbase (in millimetres) and its steering ratio (16.25 for "16.25:1"). With them, the balance read-out on an imported session says how much a corner understeers. Without them it says only which corners behave differently from the rest. Both are in the owner's manual or the manufacturer's technical data.

For the cars a track day usually sees, a catalog holds both figures, one entry per generation. In Find your car in the catalog, type a few letters ("c7", "corvette", "mx-5 nd") and pick your generation; the list shows the years.

  • A pick fills in both numbers, and the name if the car has none. The numbers stay editable, with a line saying where the catalog got them.
  • A number you had already typed is replaced only after asking, each number separately.
  • Picking a different car swaps in its numbers. Clearing the pick keeps the numbers as yours.
  • A variable-ratio rack gets no steering ratio; that one is left for you to decide.
  • Searching the catalog works offline. Saving the car, like every garage change, needs a connection.

Track Evolution can also measure the steering ratio from your own laps, pooled across your most recent sessions. It needs a wheelbase and a few imported sessions that recorded steering angle and yaw rate (a Corvette or Cadillac PDR recording does). A line then appears under the steering-ratio field:

  • Field empty — "Measured from 6 sessions: 15.8:1", with a Use this button that fills it in.
  • Field filled — a check on your number: "— matches", or "— your 12:1 is well off this; check the units".

For a rack whose ratio changes with speed, the line says so.

Track hours — the wear clock

All wear runs on on-track hours, counted from your events. An event's hours are the first of these that applies:

  1. The On-track hours you set on the event's edit form.
  2. The total of its lap times, once the event has three or more logged laps (recorded, imported or typed in).
  3. 1 hour 15 minutes per track day: about four 20-minute sessions of green-flag laps, a typical HPDE day.

Video imported from a Corvette's or Cadillac's Performance Data Recorder also carries the car's odometer. The car page then shows what the odometer said at your last recorded session, and each consumable shows, under its hours, how far the car went between the first and last recorded sessions it was fitted for. Read that distance as a floor: sessions without that video carry no reading. A reading lower than an earlier one is set aside as another car's. Wear projections run on hours only.

Consumables and wear projections

Add parts to a vehicle: brake pads, tires, rotors, brake fluid, oil. Each takes a cost and, optionally, a size, an install date and an expected life in track hours. A part on the car with no install date went on today. Tires can be one entry for a full set, or two — front tires and rear tires — for a staggered car.

Wear then accrues as you log events. Each part shows a wear bar and a projection of what's left:

  • Measured — log two or more pad-thickness or tread-depth measurements between events, and the app fits your actual wear rate per track hour and projects when you'll hit the replace-at value. This is the number to trust.
  • Expected life — with no measurements, remaining life is expected hours minus accrued hours.

Tires also count heat cycles, one per event day. For what to measure and how a wear rate is worked out, see tracking brake pads, tires and fluids on track.

Replacing a set

A part's Refresh button retires the current set and starts a new one with the same details and zero hours. The new set's expected life defaults to what your retired sets actually lasted. If the new set differs (compound, price), change it with Edit afterwards.

Retired parts have Refresh too, for another set of something you've run before: a new set with the same name, size, cost and replace-at value. Pick the install date and whether it goes straight on the car; anything it takes off moves to Spares.

Retired parts keep their history: measurements, hours lived, cost, and cost per track hour.

On the car, or on the shelf

Every part has an Equipped switch, on the web and in the iPhone and Android apps:

  • On — the part is on the car and accrues wear.
  • Off — it moves to Spares: still yours, not retired, accruing no wear.

Equipping a part takes off whatever is in its place: the other set of tires, the other pads. A full set of tires swaps out a front and a rear pair, and the other way round; a front pair leaves the rears alone. The switch asks for the date of the swap (today unless you did it earlier) and says what it will take off before it does. Adding a new part equipped works the same way.

Each part's hours, heat cycles and recorded miles count only the days it was on the car. To add a spare, untick Equipped; it needs no install date, and shows the day it was added until it has been on the car.

Swaps in the middle of a day

On one of that car's track days, a swap goes in after the last session logged so far. The part that came off keeps the sessions before it. The part that went on gets every session after, including ones you import that evening. The day's hours are shared between the two in proportion to the laps in their sessions. On any other day, the swap takes that day's date.

To correct a swap, open the part's Edit form and change when it went on the car: the date and when in the day (Start of the day, or After Session 2, say). The part it replaced moves with it. Taking a part off without a replacement asks for the date and the moment too.

What's due, and what the car has cost

  • Parts due (or nearly due) for replacement appear in a maintenance-due strip at the top of the Garage and of the car's page. Your next event names the most urgent part on the car you're taking.
  • The car's Spent tile totals every part ever fitted, retired sets included, plus what its past track days cost if you enter that on your events (see what a day cost), with the split underneath.
  • Your season's Wrapped names a favorite tire (Pro): the full set, or front or rear pair, with the most track days that year while it was equipped. Ties go to the one with more track hours.

The setup notebook

Every event carries one setup sheet per day: tire pressures (cold and hot, all four corners), camber, toe, caster, damper clicks, sway-bar settings, fuel, and which pad and tire sets were on the car.

  • Sheets copy forward: a new sheet pre-fills from your previous day, or the car's last event, and values that changed from the previous day are highlighted.
  • The notebook is on the event page on the web and in the iPhone, iPad and Android apps. With no signal, a sheet saves and syncs once you're back online.
  • It starts folded shut, showing how many day sheets it holds. Tap it to open it.

The pressure loop

A PDR recording knows the hot pressures the tires reached (see telemetry import). An imported session's Car tab lines up the cold pressures on your sheet, the measured hot pressures and your car's target hot pressure, and says what cold pressure to start from next time: "LF ran 27.0 cold → 31.3 hot; start from 25.5".

  • One button records the hot pressures on the day's sheet, with a next time note that copies forward into your next sheet.
  • On a multi-day event, another opens the next day's sheet with the suggested cold pressures filled in.

In the phone apps the loop sits under the setup notebook on the event page and reads the latest session that recorded tire pressures. For choosing pressures, see track-day tire pressures.

Setup vs. lap times

On the web, each track page ends with a Setup vs. lap times table: every sheet you've logged at that track in date order, what changed between sheets, and the event's best time and consistency. It shows what changed, not what caused a time: conditions, traffic and the driver all move lap times. Keeping a setup notebook covers what to record and how to test a change.

Private by design

Garage data — parts, wear, spend, and every setup sheet — is never included in share pages.
