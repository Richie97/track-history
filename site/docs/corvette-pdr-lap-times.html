How to get lap times from Corvette PDR video

A Corvette's Performance Data Recorder (PDR) saves the car's own data inside each video: position, speed, and what the engine, pedals and steering were doing. Track Evolution reads that data and turns each recording into a session with lap times. The video stays on your computer or phone.

What you need

  • The PDR's video files (.mp4) from the car's SD card. One recording is one session.
  • A free Track Evolution account, on the web at trackevolution.app or in the iPhone or Android app.

Nothing needs converting, and the files can be any size.

In the browser

  1. Put the PDR's SD card in your computer, or copy the videos off it.
  2. Open the event at trackevolution.app, or start a New event.
  3. Press Import video / telemetry… and choose the videos, or drag them onto the box. Pick the whole day at once: each file becomes its own session.
  4. Check the review. Each recording shows its date, length and lap count, with its laps listed.
  5. Save. The sessions and their laps are in your logbook.

On your phone

  1. Get the videos where the phone can see them, for example on a USB stick or card reader that shows up in Files.
  2. Open the event in the Track Evolution app and tap Import video, then pick the clips. Or share a clip to Track Evolution from Files (Open with on iPhone, Share on Android).
  3. Check the review and save.

Nothing is copied, so a large file needs no free space on the phone.

Where the lap times come from

From the recorder's own start/finish markers, to the millisecond. Where the recorder missed a crossing, the lap is worked out from the distance driven and marked with a ~. Estimated laps are usually within a few tenths of a second.

A recording with no markers shows the track map you drove. Click where the start/finish line is and laps are timed on each pass across it. One click covers every file in the import. See the start/finish line picker.

What you get

  • Lap times for every full lap, and the session's best.
  • Your racing line for the best lap, as a track map.
  • Top speed, max RPM and max lateral G for the session.
  • Graphs for every lap, overlaid by distance around the track: speed, throttle, brake, steering, RPM, gear, cornering and braking force, and where ABS or traction control cut in.
  • The car's health, lap by lap: oil, coolant and transmission temperatures, oil pressure, tire pressures and temperatures, fuel and battery voltage.
  • The outside temperature the car measured.

What is free and what is Pro

  • Free, with no limit on files: importing, the lap times, the racing line, the session's top figures, and the speed, throttle and brake graphs for every lap.
  • Track Evolution Pro ($1.99 a month or $19.99 a year): the other graphs, sector splits and a theoretical best lap, lap-against-lap time deltas, the friction circle, the car-health readings and best-lap video (a clip of your fastest lap, or of every lap, kept with the session).

If it doesn't work

  • The review shows a map and no laps. The recording has no start/finish markers. Click the line on the map.
  • "No laps cross the picked line". Click a part of the track the car drove on every lap.
  • "No laps found". The recording has no markers and no repeating lap, usually paddock or pit-lane footage. Leave it out.
  • Some laps have a ~. The recorder missed a crossing and those laps were estimated. Nothing to fix.
  • "No PDR, SmartyCam or GoPro telemetry in this video". The file has no data track. Re-encoding, trimming in an editor or downloading from a sharing site removes it; use the original from the SD card.
