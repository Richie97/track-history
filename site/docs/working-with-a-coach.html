Getting the most from an instructor or coach

An instructor in the passenger seat is the fastest way to improve that a track day offers, and in the novice group it usually comes with the entry fee. How much you get from it depends a good deal on you: what you tell them, how you listen, and what you do with it afterward.

Before the day

  • Be honest about your experience. How many days, at which tracks, in what car. Instructors plan around what you tell them, and overstating it makes the day worse and less safe.
  • Describe the car. Power, tires, brakes, and anything unusual about it — including driver aids and whether you normally leave them on.
  • Say what you want to work on. One or two things: "braking later with confidence", "the fast right-hander", "being smoother". "Go faster" is not a goal an instructor can use.
  • Mention anything that worries you — a corner, the wet, traffic. They would much rather know.

In the car

  • Agree how you will communicate before you leave the pits: hand signals, an intercom, what "brake" and "wait" will mean.
  • Do what is asked, even when it feels slow. An instructor often slows you down to fix a line or an input, and the speed comes back with interest.
  • One thing at a time. Trying to act on five instructions in one lap means none of them stick.
  • Ask for a demonstration. Many instructors will drive your car for a few laps, or take you out in theirs. Seeing the braking point from the passenger seat is worth a lot of explaining.
  • Say when you are tired or overloaded. Coming in a lap early costs nothing.

Between sessions

The debrief is where the lesson is made permanent. Do it straight away, before the next group goes out:

  1. Ask for the one or two things that would make the biggest difference next session.
  2. Repeat them back in your own words.
  3. Write them down, by corner.
  4. Ask what they would work on if they were you.

If you have lap times or data from the session, look at them together. An instructor can read a speed trace in seconds and tell you which difference matters.

After the day

  • Write up the day that evening: what you were told, what worked, what to start with next time.
  • Start the next event from those notes, and show them to your next instructor. A different instructor each event is common, and without notes each one starts from zero.
  • Keep working with the same coach if you can. Someone who has seen several of your days can tell a habit from a one-off.

What a coach wants to see

Away from the circuit, a coach can do a great deal with very little:

  • your lap times for each session, not only the best one
  • video of a lap, ideally with the speed shown
  • your speed, brake and throttle traces, if you have them — see reading brake and throttle traces
  • which corners you find hardest, in your words

Sharing your logbook with a coach

Track Evolution can give an instructor or coach read-only access to your whole logbook: every event, session and lap, the channel graphs and sector splits, your racing lines, your best-lap videos, and your cars with their modifications. They can prepare before the day instead of looking at your phone in the paddock.

  1. Open Coaching — from the account menu on the web, or under Settings in the phone apps — and create an invite link.
  2. Send it, or let your coach scan its QR code. The link works once and for seven days.
  3. They sign in and accept. A free account is enough for the coach.

A driver profile on the same page — your experience, your safety gear and what you want to work on — is the first thing they see, which covers most of "before the day" above. Your coach can also leave comments on a session, a lap or a marked place on a lap, and you can reply; comments are in the web app and the Android app today.

They never see your email, notes, prep checklists, costs, setup sheets or garage parts, they cannot change anything, and you can remove them at any time. Sharing with a coach is part of Track Evolution Pro for the driver.
